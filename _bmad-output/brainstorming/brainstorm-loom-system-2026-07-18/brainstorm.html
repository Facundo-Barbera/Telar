I · JOB TO BE DONE

The hiring

Before designing anything: what is a loom hired to do? The answer came by listing everything already fired.

  • bmad — plans and executes beautifully, but testing is weak and no project ever closed.user
  • raw harnesses — great at doing, weak at final delivery; they demand constant steering.user
  • dynamic workflows — "great for automating development… but in a sense, not complete."user
  • the loom — hired for the one thing none of them finish: delivery you can trust, without steering.
“When I know what I want built, I hire a loom to carry it from intent to proven-working feature — judgment, not steering.” job statement · the deliverable is evidence; code is the side effect

Three boundaries drew themselves: simple tasks never get loomsuser · fleets are the norm — seven epics at once, an inbox of proven workuser · and the pipeline dream: looms build what's planned while the planner keeps planning.user

II · TRAIT TRANSFER

Three anchors, three grafts

Name what makes each inspiration work — the trait beneath the feature — then graft it onto the loom.

bmad

the method

Every planning artifact has a specialist author — and after planning, automation takes the baton.user

telar's exit is pluralistic but its entrance is monolithic — staff the intake with seats; make the entrance match the exit. And since telar owns the platform: the method becomes structural, enforced by code, not discipline.

ultracode

the workflows

Deterministic spine, intelligence in the leaves — and above all, parallelization, with planning first.user

parallelism is a planning output, not a runtime feature: interference analysis shapes decomposition; fleet level gets lanes + a landing queue.

Codex Ultras

the delegation

Context preservation — subdivide instead of bombing one agent; “make sure your agent has everything they need.”user

materialized as the context manifest on every node of a compiled flow — decided at plan-time, so nobody drowns and nobody starves.
“The loom is not only failing in design — it's failing in preparation.” the reframe that reorganized the whole session · user

From here the fork appeared — pre-defined steps (bmad-like) or orchestrator-defined (ultra-like)? — and dissolved: dynamic at plan-time, deterministic at run-time. The thread compiles its flow: a validated DAG with parallel lanes and dependency edges, executed bindingly, recompiled only as an explicit audited event.coach Which set up the day's central decision: the core idea — problem definition comes from the user; a preparation graph grows where drift exists; then the orchestrator alone carries vision to reality, and conductors never touch code.user

INTERLUDE

The detour that drew the graph

Mid-session, words weren't enough — so the preparation graph became a picture: preparation-graph.html, styled with the app's own tokens. Three versions later it was true. The corrections were the point:

  • “This is not a weave — a weave is already planned. This comes before the loom.” user

  • The automatic write-back arc — killed. “Map updates are work; work goes through looms; nobody writes the map silently.” Adopted instead: drift ledger + lazy intake diff, with a threshold-triggered reconciliation loom as escalation. user
III · ASSUMPTION REVERSAL

The flip table

Every redesign drags invisible assumptions. Each got flipped; what fell out stayed.

A loom runs to completion, then you judge.
→
Evidence streams; judging mid-flight is possible.
But refined by the user: watching must be a choice, never an obligation — evidence is a window, escalation is a doorbell, and the doorbell rings only for dire. At fleet scale, that's the difference between an inbox and notification hell.
One loom = one intent.
→
A loom carries a batch; multi-task is core to the vision.
The flip was already the design — clarified so no future agent reads "loom = single task" from fresh context. A loom is sized by its accept moment, not its intent count.
Preparation happens before execution.
→
The inversion is the current, broken state — quick specs, starved context, incomplete tasks.
And it produced the day's most concrete new node: verification-readiness — prove the lab works before the experiment; its recipe lives in the map and compounds. No lab, no experiment.
“Getting this done.”
⊥
Unflippable. Inverted, the loom shouldn't exist.
The flip test separates axioms (flip = nonexistence) from assumptions (flip = ideas). Everything else was furniture; this is the foundation.
IV · KILL THE CROWN JEWEL

What the moat is made of

The human-accept moat — deleted, hypothetically. Looms merging themselves overnight. What could possibly replace the gate?

“The only thing that could replace it is an agent that totally understands the product's vision… an agent that could do my job.”

Not better tests — correctness is already delegated. Not more evidence — the cards carry it. The moat's true material is vision: the accept moment is the one place the product's why enters the system. The jewel went back. What stayed: a roadmap — externalize judgment into the map's objective and form regions, and one day a vision critic pre-judges each delivery, advisory only, making the click faster and the gap measurable.

SYNTHESIS

The loom, in three acts

Not five systems — one idea, applied three times.

act i

Prepare

A conversation, a diff against the living map, planning nodes spawning only where drift exists — ending at a gate only you open: accept, modify, or deny.

act ii

Execute

The orchestrator conducts created → proven → delivered. Threads compile their own flows and run them deterministically. Conductors never touch the instruments.

act iii

Judge

An inbox of delivery cards — claim, proof, narrative, risk. Windows always open; the doorbell reserved for dire. Your click is the only door out.

declare → validate → execute deterministically → reconcile lazily  ·  the fractal running through all three acts

  • wall 1Verifiers can't write — a verdict can't be self-issued.
  • wall 2Conductors can't code — the plan can't become the implementation.
  • wall 3Agents can't accept — delivery can't be self-declared.
  • wall 4Nobody writes the map silently — knowledge gets the same moat as code.

Every wall, gate, and graph exists so that the human stays in the planner seat.

The last thread, caught in the mirror: pause / resume. Credits run out; a loom that dies at 80% fails the axiom. Under the old design the feature was impossible; under the new one — explicit state, compiled flows — it's nearly free. The redesign proved itself on its way out the door.user
